About This Service

Apostille on an FBI Criminal Background Check

If you're applying for a job, residency permit, or long-term visa in a country that has signed The Hague Convention, your FBI Background Check needs an official Apostille from the U.S. Department of State before most foreign governments will accept it. That covers South Korea, Spain, Italy, Germany, France, and 120+ other countries.

We're a licensed agency in the Washington DC area. We've been handling apostilles since 2008, and we file your documents in person at the Department of State — not by mail, not through a secondary intermediary. In-person submission is faster and removes any risk of documents being rejected because of postal delays or mailing errors.

Most clients handle the whole process remotely. Send us your FBI report as a PDF, we review it at no charge, confirm exactly what's needed, and give you a firm quote with a clear timeline before you're charged anything.

How It Works

Three Simple Steps — We Handle Everything

1

Get Your FBI Background Check

Before we can process your apostille, you'll need an official FBI Identity History Summary (Criminal Background Check). There are two ways to get one:

Option A — Directly through the FBI: Visit fbi.gov and navigate to the Identity History Summary Checks section. Submit your request online. The FBI delivers results as a PDF in most cases.

Option B — Through an FBI-approved Channeler: Channelers are private companies the FBI has authorized to accept fingerprints and submit background check requests on your behalf, often with faster turnaround than going directly through the FBI. The full list of approved Channelers is on the FBI website.

💡 Results from both the FBI and approved Channelers are accepted. Already have your PDF? Go straight to step two.
2

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About FBI Background Check Apostilles

What is an apostille for an FBI Background Check?

An apostille is an internationally recognized certification issued by the U.S. Department of State that verifies the authenticity of your FBI Background Check for use in countries that have signed the 1961 Hague Convention. It's required for international employment applications, residency permits, and visa processes in member countries.

Do I need an apostille or embassy authentication?

If your destination country is a Hague Convention member, you need an apostille. If it's not a member, your document needs embassy authentication (also called legalization). Not sure which applies? Contact us — we'll confirm at no charge.

How long does the FBI apostille process take?

With our Express service, the total process takes 8–9 business days from the time we receive your documents. Standard runs 12–14 business days, and Economy is available for clients with flexible timelines — 5+ weeks. Keep in mind that Fridays do not count as working days at the U.S. Department of State.

I already have my FBI Background Check PDF. Can I send it directly?

Yes — email the PDF to info@usauthentication.com or upload it through our online order form. We'll review it at no cost and confirm next steps before you're charged anything.

Can you apostille an FBI Background Check for employment in South Korea, Spain, or Italy?

Yes. South Korea, Spain, Italy, and most countries where US residents seek international employment are Hague Convention members. We process apostilles for all member nations. If you're unsure whether your destination requires an apostille or embassy authentication, reach out — we'll tell you for free.

Which countries do you serve?

We process FBI Background Check apostilles for all 120+ Hague Convention member nations, including South Korea, Spain, Italy, Germany, France, Brazil, and many more. If your country isn't a Hague member, we also handle embassy authentication.
