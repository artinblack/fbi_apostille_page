Apostille for FBI Background Check

An apostille on a FBI background check may be needed if you are you are planning to apply for residency permits, long-term visas, obtain foreign citizenship, or employment abroad. If your destination is a member of the Hague Convention, your FBI Background Check needs an apostille from the U.S. Department of State . The Hague convention consists of countries such as South Korea, Spain, Italy, Colombia, France, and 120+ other countries.

What is an Apostille? Why do I need it?

An apostille is a certificate that "legalizes" or "authenticates" a document for use outside of the United States. In the Apostille process a document receives a special page which has the issuing agency's signature, stamp, and seal. Apostille pages can be issued from either the state or federal level, depending on where the document issuing agency resides. For documents like the FBI background check, the Apostille is issued by the US Department of State. The Apostille certificate is not accepted in every country, but only those that are part of an agreement called the Hague Convention. The Hague convention consists of over 120+ countries who all agree to use the Apostille certificate as the simplified process for accepting foreign documents for use in their country. What is the FBI Background check? How do I obtain one?

The FBI background check, sometimes called the FBI Identity History Summary or FBI rap sheet, is a collection of an individual's criminal activities and arrest history in the US at a State and Federal level. If there are no such records, a statement of no prior arrest data will be will be issued. The FBI Background Check is commonly required for a variety of reasons by the destination country to prove the individual has no serious criminal history. Common reasons for needing a FBI background check are employment, international business, studying abroad, and visa or residency applications. Getting Your FBI Background Check

Before we can process your apostille, you'll need an official FBI Identity History Summary (also called a Criminal Background Check). There are two ways to get one:

Option A — Directly through the FBI: Visit fbi.gov and navigate to the Identity History Summary Checks section. Submit your request online. The FBI delivers results as a PDF in most cases.

Option B — Through an FBI-approved Channeler: Channelers are private companies the FBI has authorized to accept fingerprints and submit background check requests on your behalf, often with faster turnaround than going directly through the FBI. The full list of approved Channelers is on the FBI website.

Results from both sources are accepted. Once you have your PDF, email it to us and we'll confirm it's suitable for apostille — at no cost.

Common Questions

What is an apostille for an FBI Background Check?

An apostille is an internationally recognized certification issued by the U.S. Department of State that verifies the authenticity of your FBI Background Check for use in countries that have signed the 1961 Hague Convention. It's required for international employment applications, residency permits, and visa processes in member countries.

Do I need an apostille or embassy authentication?

If your destination country is a Hague Convention member, you need an apostille. If it's not a member, your document needs embassy authentication (also called legalization). Not sure which applies? Contact us — we'll confirm at no charge.
